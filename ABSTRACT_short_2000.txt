Four assumptions are examined: that a monotone co-expression metric captures functional relatedness; that a global cut yields reproducible modules; that a single partition resolves nested sub-structure; and that reported modularity reflects biological organisation. All four fail. We assembled a benchmark of 16 publicly available expression cohorts spanning neoplastic and non-neoplastic disease, nine GO biological-process pathways and 31 partitioning algorithms from eight methodological families, yielding 100 pathway-cohort pairs and 3100 evaluation records. Motivated by the finding that expression graphs degrade under realistic constraints, we further introduce FACEO-R, a functionally anchored overlapping community detection method in which the weight of GO-derived functional similarity adapts to the estimated reliability of the expression graph. Under controlled degradation (sample subsampling, expression noise and feature dropout) and on clean data alike, FACEO-R outperformed Leiden at gamma = 1.0 in 118/145 comparisons (81%; paired Wilcoxon p = 1.9e-14) and WGCNA-style hierarchical cutting in 127/145 (88%; p = 9.0e-20), with the largest gains under feature dropout (alpha = +0.070, 27/28 wins). Conclusions. We find that the four implicit assumptions of current practice - monotone functional relatedness, a globally valid cut, a single defensible resolution, and modularity as evidence - all fail, and that the failures are systematic. Availability and implementation: The FACEO-R implementation, all analysis scripts, processed expression matrices and complete evaluation tables are freely available at https://github.com/sunmy1129/faceo-r under an MIT licence.
